About

Who I am

I'm a Senior Lecturer of Accountancy at the Gies College of Business, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I teach data-driven decision making, Python for business analytics, and accounting analytics — helping students turn raw data into decisions that auditors, controllers, and analysts can actually act on.

Before Illinois, I worked as a chief data officer at Gigg and as a chief data scientist at Numetric where I worked with clients including the New York State and Utah State Departments of Transportation, Orbit, Lenovo, and Standard Plumbing. I've alsotaught at Rice University, Georgia State University, and as a visiting professor at Washington University in St. Louis's Olin Business School.

I hold a Ph.D. in Accounting from the University of Iowa and a B.B.A. and M.Acc. in Accounting from Brigham Young University.

I've done a little bit of research that has appeared in the Journal of Management Accounting Research and Accounting Horizons. I write textbooks, build classroom tools, and look for any excuse to find data-driven solutions.

Photo Mosaic of Role Models

Python NumPy Pillow Crowdsourced data

Every semester I ask students who their role models are. This portrait is from my Fall 2026 MBA 561 class. It's built entirely out of their answers — about 150 names, from "father" and "mother" to Steve Jobs, Michelle Obama, and Warren Buffett — each rendered as a photo tile and color-matched to reconstruct the target image. A custom "repeat penalty" in the matching algorithm makes an already-used tile look progressively farther away in color the more it's picked, so the mosaic draws from the full breadth of names submitted instead of leaning on whichever few tiles are the closest overall match.

Built as a classroom data-storytelling exercise. Individual tile photos were retrieved via automated web image search and remain the property of their original owners/photographers.

ChoiceCoach

Google AI Studio Analytic Hierarchy Process Decision Analysis

A decision-coaching app I built in Google AI Studio using the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) — you lay out a goal, the objectives that matter for it, and the options you're weighing, and it uses pairwise comparisons to weight each objective and score every option against them, turning a fuzzy decision into a structured, defensible one.

Interests

Some things I like

Outside of what I've built for class, I like following new ways to tell a data story. Scrollytelling — tying an animation or visualization directly to the reader's scroll position — is one of my favorites. It forces a complex idea into small, sequential beats instead of one dense chart, turning "here's a figure" into a guided walkthrough. That's exactly the instinct I want students to build: don't just show the data, walk someone through it.

Inside a Neural Network

D3.js Scrollytelling Interactive

A scroll-driven walkthrough of how a small multilayer perceptron actually learns — forward passes, activation functions, loss, and backpropagation — built entirely in D3, ending with a live network training in the browser in front of you on a two-moons dataset.
